Face-Tracking Camera Turret

Completed

Technologies & Tools: Python, OpenCV, Arduino (C++), 3D Printing (CAD), Closed-Loop Control, Serial Communication, External Power Electronics

Designed and built a 2-axis (pan-tilt) computer-vision camera turret that detects and tracks a human face in real time. The system splits duties between a PC (OpenCV face detection) and an Arduino Uno (microcontroller motor control) linked via USB serial communication.

System Architecture & Hardware Design

  • Dual-System Architecture: Offloaded heavy vision processing to Python/OpenCV while running real-time servo timing and slew-rate limiting on the Arduino Uno.
  • Custom 3D-Printed Mount: Designed a custom pan-tilt bracket assembly to securely mount the USB camera and high-torque SG90 positional servos.
  • Power Isolation: Powered the servos via an isolated external 5V power supply to prevent micro-controller brownouts and current spikes under high load.
  • Proportional Control & Dead-Zone Logic: Implemented proportional angle adjustments based on frame offsets, paired with an independent X/Y dead zone to prevent endless micro-jitter.

Real-World Engineering Challenges & Mechanical Tuning

  • Camera Weight & Mechanical Drag: The heavy camera mass caused severe servo drag and structural sag. Solved by physically mounting the base to a wood foundation, recalibrating the tracking speed/gain, and adjusting code parameters to account for gravity drag.
  • Momentum & Over-shooting: Fast face tracking caused heavy inertia, making servos fly past targets. Added **slew-rate limiting** in the Arduino sketch to step motor angles gradually (preventing mechanical shear) and slowed down decision intervals to 10 Hz.
  • OpenCV Cascade Gotcha: Resolved broken cascade builds by pinning software releases strictly to stable releases (`4.10.0.84`).
